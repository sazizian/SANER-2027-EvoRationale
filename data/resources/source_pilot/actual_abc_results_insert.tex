\subsection{Controlled Structural-Policy Study}
\label{sec:controlled-abc-actual}
We executed three conditions on the same 298 frozen repository cases:
A retained the decision inventory and enforced the structural contracts;
B retained the inventory and recorded contract violations diagnostically;
C used three stages without the inventory. All conditions used the same
model, reasoning setting, stage count, and per-request output limit.
A and B shared prompt templates, while C also changed the inter-stage
representation and instructions. All conditions retained the common
verified-context filter. Thus, B--C evaluates an inventory-and-representation
bundle rather than removal of every source-validation mechanism.

Condition A completed 259 cases on first attempts and 293 after bounded
retries. B completed all 298 cases on first attempts; C completed 297,
with one HTTP failure subsequently recovered. A's 55 rejected stage attempts
comprised 16 Stage~1 and 39 Stage~3 rejections. Stage~3 trace failures occurred
in 39 attempts, including 23 with coverage failures. Stage~1 provenance and
inventory failures occurred in 14 and 15 attempts, respectively; these
categories overlap. Five A cases remained unavailable after the retry limit.

Enforcement limited release to outputs satisfying the implemented
structural predicates. All 1,341 recommendations in A's accepted final
outputs passed the structural trace check. This is a release-policy property,
not a semantic-correctness assessment. Applying the A predicates to B's
fixed archived chains would accept 257 and reject 41. This retrospective
replay measures policy compliance of existing content; it does not recreate
the generation that would follow an enforced rejection.

The realized computation differed despite common request settings.
A consumed 7.1\% more recorded tokens than B and 22.9\% more than C.
One C error has no reported usage, so its token total covers available
records rather than certified complete billable consumption. These results
characterize structural compliance, availability, and operational cost;
they do not establish a semantic advantage independent of computation.

\subsection{Source-Only Human Pilot}
\label{sec:abc-source-pilot}
Two assessors returned source annotations for the six calibration cases,
covering two discussions, two issues, and two pull requests. They submitted
37 and 35 concern annotations, respectively. Offline checks found all 72
quoted excerpts verbatim within the cited source-line spans. The counts
describe annotations over the same six cases, not distinct cases or an
adjudicated set of unique concerns. Literal quotation checks do not establish
semantic entailment or completeness of the extracted concerns.

Participation ended after source annotation. No comparative judgments of
the new A/B/C outputs were collected, and the planned 60-case main ABC human
study was not completed. The original submissions were retained unchanged.
Assistance declarations were incomplete or ambiguous; the coordinator
reported writing assistance for the first assessor, but the workbook wording
did not cleanly separate writing assistance from substantive annotation.
This pilot is consequently reported as limited source-annotation evidence,
separately from the earlier comparative human study.
